/02 [ A WORKED EXAMPLE ]

Laundry, 5:30 PM.

We round each cost to the cent first, then show the saving as the difference: 57¢ − 27¢ =Save about 30¢
When a run crosses a boundary, each minute is priced in its own period. The dishwasher (1.2 kWh over 2 hours) started at 5:30 PM spends 90 minutes on-peak and 30 minutes off-peak: 0.9 kWh × 18.9¢ + 0.3 kWh × 9.1¢ = 19.7¢, shown as 20¢.
/03 [ THE SEARCH ]

Every 15 minutes, next 24 hours.

Ties go to the earliest

If two start times cost the same to the cent, we pick the earlier one. You get your chores done sooner.

Deadlines and quiet hours

A "must be done by" time removes any start that would finish late. Quiet hours remove starts while you're asleep. Delay-start appliances can still run overnight.

When timing does not matter, we say so

On weekends and holidays (TOU), and always on Tiered, the answer is "Any time. Same price all day." We never invent a saving.

Holidays, 2026

Jan 1, Feb 16, Apr 3, May 18, Jul 1, Aug 3, Sep 7, Oct 12, Dec 25 and Dec 28 (Boxing Day, observed). TOU is off-peak all day. ULO uses weekend off-peak plus ultra-low overnight. When a holiday falls on a weekend, the next weekday that isn't a holiday gets holiday prices.

/04 [ WHAT WE DON'T KNOW ]

Estimates, not a bill.

{{l.p}}

Every saving is an estimate of the electricity cost only. Delivery, regulatory charges, HST and the Ontario Electricity Rebate are not included.

/05 [ WHOLESALE VS YOUR BILL ]

The Grid page isn't your bill.

Homes on the Regulated Price Plan pay fixed prices by time period. The wholesale market price shows how stressed Ontario's system is, but it doesn't change what you pay. Wherever we show it, it is labelled: "Wholesale market price. Not what you pay."
